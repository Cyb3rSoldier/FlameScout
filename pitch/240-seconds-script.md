## 0:45–1:45 · WHY — Empathy for the problem (~150 words)

> [No repetition of the cabin scenario — the hook already put us there. Pick up *inside* that fire.]
>
> That floating flame is a real, unanswered safety problem. On Earth, convection shapes the fire and firefighters arrive. Aboard a spacecraft in transit, there are no firefighters, no fire brigade, no nearest exit — and the flame behaves in ways our detector and extinguisher designs on Earth never trained for.
>
> Every answer NASA needs — what burns, how fast, at what oxygen and pressure — already exists, buried in thirty years of microgravity combustion experiments: FLEX, BASS, Saffire, SAME. But the data lives in dozens of reports, tables, and PDFs that don't talk to each other.
>
> Here's our killer data point: when we mapped every tested condition on a pressure-versus-oxygen grid, the regime future crews will actually live in — Moon-base atmosphere, thirty percent oxygen at ten psi — came back **empty**. Not thin. Empty.
>
> Mission designers are engineering around a silent corner. That's the gap we built for.

## 1:45–2:45 · WHAT — The innovation (~150 words)

> FlameScout is a web dashboard that turns NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics — the PSI — into clear, cited answers. Everything on screen traces back to a real source, and when the evidence is thin, the app says so.
>
> **Mission Fit** ranks every investigation against your crewed environment — spacecraft, lunar base, pressurized rover, spacesuit — with transparent weights you can read, no black box.
>
> **Predict** estimates how a burning fuel droplet behaves — built from **280 real FLEX tests, NASA Technical Paper 2015-216046, Table IX** — and it refuses to answer outside the tested range. Honest by design.
>
> The **Experiment Lab** lets you grab a slider and raise oxygen or drop pressure, and watch which corners of the map have data and which don't.
>
> And **Safety Controls** groups the research into prevention, detection, and suppression — the studies that inform the next generation of fire-safety equipment.
